## Section 2 — Innovation Overview

**Innovation name:** AquaGuardian AI

**One-line summary:** A closed-loop engineering platform that simulates,
stress-tests, and validates every water-infrastructure decision — leak
isolation, irrigation, pump load, fire response — before it reaches physical
equipment.

**Track:** A (TRL 1–3) · **Category:** Digitalisation, Automation and Smart
Water Management

**Water challenge addressed:** Water infrastructure operates reactively —
leaks are discovered after significant loss, pump failures after service
interruption, irrigation on fixed schedules rather than measured plant stress.
Existing monitoring and predictive-analytics tools detect and predict, but
rarely simulate and stress-test a candidate response against multiple
conditions before it is executed. AquaGuardian adds that missing validation
step: "Decision Before Execution."

## Section 3 — Technical Questions (Track A: TRL 1–3)

**Scientific/technical principle:** Every candidate action a water system
could take (isolate a valve, throttle a pump, start irrigation, dispatch a
fire response) is run through a deterministic closed-loop cycle — Sense,
Analyze, Simulate, Stress Test, Optimize, Validate, Execute — inside a digital
twin before being approved. If a plan fails stress testing under adversarial
conditions (e.g. closing a valve that would starve a downstream sector), it is
rejected, automatically re-optimized, and re-tested. Only a plan that clears
every constraint reaches Execute.

**What's new relative to the state of the art:** Existing SCADA/threshold
monitoring and predictive-analytics tools detect and sometimes predict
failures, but candidate-action simulation and stress testing are typically
external, manual, or absent — plan rejection is operator-dependent rather than
an explicit, evidenced policy. AquaGuardian makes simulate-before-execute a
first-class, automated step with a machine-readable decision trail.

**Evidence to date:** A working deterministic software prototype covering five
water-risk scenarios (pipeline leak, pump degradation, water contamination,
drought stress, wildfire risk), each run under four stress profiles (nominal
through extreme, including sensor noise and communication loss). Comparative
evidence against a reactive baseline and a detection-only-AI baseline shows,
averaged across scenarios: a 74% reduction in water loss, a 99.9% reduction in
response delay, and a stress-test pass rate of 0% (reactive) vs. 100%
(AquaGuardian's selected plan). All figures are generated by the included
deterministic simulator (`aquaguardian/comparison.py`), reproducible via the
project's automated test suite, and explicitly labeled as simulated PoC
results, not field-trial data. One per-scenario metric (nominal safety-risk
score, pump-degradation scenario) does not improve under the closed loop and
is disclosed rather than omitted — see `docs/COMPARATIVE_VALIDATION.md`.

The platform additionally bundles `hexa_tpu_rt_sim`, an independently built
edge/network/MEC architecture simulator, and calls it live (not as a cited
constant) to validate the "sub-1ms local decision" claim underlying the edge
layer: 0 deadline misses across 200 samples against a 1ms hard deadline, and a
network layer modeled as 5G URLLC meeting a 10ms SLA at p99 ≈ 4.2ms.

**Pathway to prototype/pilot:** TRL 4 requires connecting real pressure, flow,
soil-moisture, and vibration sensors; replaying recorded fault traces through
the engine; calibrating the digital twin against measured behavior; and
verifying fail-safe behavior under sensor noise, packet loss, and actuator
unavailability. TRL 5–6 requires a shadow-mode pilot alongside an operating
farm, irrigation district, or small water utility (no actuator commands),
followed by a limited supervised pilot on low-risk actions once acceptance
criteria are met. Key technical risks: calibration accuracy against a real
hydraulic network, sensor/actuator integration reliability, and OT security
posture for any eventual actuator-control path — all named explicitly in
`docs/TRL_ASSESSMENT.md` and `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` rather than assumed away.

## Section 4 — Impact & Market

**Scale of the challenge:** By GPIW's own framing, 2.1 billion people lack
access to safe drinking water, global water demand is projected to rise
20–30% by 2050, and agriculture consumes roughly 70% of global freshwater.
AquaGuardian targets the operational layer beneath those numbers: avoidable
water loss from undetected leaks, fixed-schedule irrigation waste, and
reactive (rather than predictive) infrastructure maintenance.

**Target beneficiaries:** Beachhead — commercial farms and irrigation
districts (drought resilience, irrigation optimization, pump reliability,
leak containment). Expansion — municipal distribution utilities
(non-revenue-water reduction, pressure management, auditable incident
response) and reservoirs/dams/treatment facilities (equipment reliability,
water-quality event handling).

**Competitive landscape:** AquaGuardian does not replace SCADA, hydraulic
modeling, or human operators — it is positioned as a validation and
decision-support layer that integrates with them. Relative to SCADA/threshold
monitoring and predictive-analytics tools, its differentiator is making
candidate-action simulation, stress testing, and unsafe-plan rejection
explicit, automated, and evidenced rather than manual, occasional, or
operator-dependent (full comparison table in `docs/MARKET_ANALYSIS.md`).

**Barriers to adoption:** Named explicitly rather than minimized — utility and
OT integration is conservative by necessity; the platform currently has no
field validation, no design-partner letter of intent, and no independent
security audit. These are the exact gaps a GPIW pilot pathway would close.
