VERDICT:
Unlike the CNN benchmark, the memory-blind formula does NOT show a binary
cliff here -- it degrades gradually (misses fall smoothly as width increases).
That means the Phase 1.5/2 'total livelock' cliff is a property of UNIFORM,
SYMMETRIC demand, not a general property of the memory-blind formula -- a real
workload with mixed task sizes fails more gracefully even without the fix.

The memory-aware fix still helps substantially (fewer misses at every width
below the full-bandwidth point), but does NOT fully eliminate misses the way
it did on the CNN benchmark. Residual misses concentrate specifically in QKV
projection tasks -- the largest per-head task, and the first op dispatched in
each block, when all 8 heads' AXI requests ramp up together faster than the
assignment-time snapshot estimate can foresee. This is the concrete case behind
the caveat already flagged in Phase 2: 'estimate is fixed at assignment, blind
to contention that hasn't started yet.' The fix is real and substantial, but
not complete -- Phase 7's adaptive re-estimation is what would close this gap.
